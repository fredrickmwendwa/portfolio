About

A backend developer who thinks about how things look.

Not two careers running in parallel - one way of working, applied to both code and design.

Fredrick Mwendwa
Nairobi, Kenya

I'm Fredrick Mwendwa. I'm in my final year of a Computer Science diploma at Kiambu National Polytechnic, and alongside that I build backend systems in Python and Django, and design brand identities and social content for small businesses around me.

The design side came first, honestly - before I could explain what a foreign key was, I was already thinking about spacing, contrast, and why one logo felt more trustworthy than another. Backend development came later, through coursework and then through actually wanting to build things that worked end to end, not just look right on a slide.

I don't treat the two as separate skill sets I switch between. Both are about the same thing: taking something unclear and making it legible - whether that's a database schema or a business's visual identity.

"Good backend work is invisible. Good design is the first thing you notice. I like that they need opposite instincts."

Most of the people I've built for so far are small businesses around Nairobi, figuring out their systems and their brand at the same time, usually with a limited budget and no patience for anything over-engineered. That constraint has taught me more about practical software and design decisions than any course has.

How I approach the work

01

Start with the actual problem

Before I open Django or Figma, I want to know exactly what's breaking or missing - not what's trendy to build.

02

Simple, until it can't be

I'd rather ship something plain that works than something clever that's fragile. Complexity has to earn its place.

03

Design decisions need reasons

Every color, spacing choice, or layout decision should be defensible - not "it looked fine."

04

Finish the boring parts

Error states, empty states, edge cases, export margins - the unglamorous 20% is usually where quality actually shows.
